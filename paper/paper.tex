\begin{abstract}
Rhythm-game charts are designed artifacts with many valid realizations
per song, yet generated charts are still evaluated with metrics borrowed
from other tasks: F-measure against one reference chart, language-model
(LM) perplexity, and unreferenced statistics such as entropy,
distinct-$n$, and self-similarity. We validate metrics by measuring
their response to controlled damage. Applying nine targeted corruption
operators at increasing doses to 170 human charts (4{,}760 scored
variants), we find failures that spot checks do not reveal: perplexity
\emph{improves} by 37\% when charts are flattened toward the LM's modal
patterns, self-similarity structureness \emph{rises} by 62\% under
copy-paste collapse, and all 56 chart-only quantities we compute are
insensitive to a global 60\,ms audio misalignment (maximum response
0.03 standard deviations)---a displacement ten times the perceptual
threshold. A two-layer protocol repairs these failures: raw diagnostics
are normalized against per-difficulty target bands calibrated on the
official-chart corpus, and a perceptually anchored timing family---a
6\,ms just-noticeable-difference deadzone, severity tiers at its
multiples, and a fixed-lattice phase detector over a two-tier
grid-reference hierarchy---recovers injected 15/30/60\,ms shifts with
exact medians on at least 99.4\% of charts, without a reference chart.
The corruptions also establish complementarity: a sparse-outlier
corruption that grid matching largely absorbs is detected at the token
level by the grammar family, while the anchor shift that no chart-only
metric registers is detected only by the timing family---the score
profile cannot be reduced to a scalar. Applied to five published
generators across three chart vocabularies, the profile replaces
single-number rankings with mechanism-level diagnoses. We release the
corruption operators, calibration artifacts, per-chart records, and an
installable evaluation package. \PENDING{P8: artifact URL.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Automatic chart generation for rhythm games---mapping audio to a
playable sequence of timed, typed notes---has progressed from
onset-detection pipelines to conditional sequence
models~\cite{donahue2017ddc,halina2021taikonation,takada2023genelive,
hanzen2025tcp}. Evaluation has not kept pace. Because a chart is a
designed artifact rather than an annotation, the same song admits many
valid charts, and the field's dominant tool---F-measure against the
single official chart---penalizes every valid alternative equally and
cannot be computed for songs without charts. The usual complement is
reference-free measurement, for which authors borrow from symbolic
music and text generation: LM perplexity~\cite{donahue2017ddc},
pattern-space congruence~\cite{halina2021taikonation}, event entropy,
distinct-$n$~\cite{li2016diversity}, Self-BLEU~\cite{zhu2018texygen},
feature-distribution comparison~\cite{yang2020evaluation,dong2020muspy},
and self-similarity structureness~\cite{wu2020jazz}.

This paper asks whether those borrowed metrics measure chart quality,
and answers with a validation method that requires no listening test:
\emph{targeted corruption}. Starting from human (official) charts, we
apply nine damage operators---dense timing jitter, sparse severe
outliers, global anchor shift, color shuffling, copy-paste collapse,
LM-argmax flattening, density scaling, burst insertion, bar
shuffling---at three doses each. On average, corrupting a human chart
cannot make it better, so a usable quality metric must respond in the
correct direction, and the response must grow with dose. Metric
validation becomes an automated, reproducible procedure that requires
neither human raters nor model training, and applies unchanged to any
proposed chart metric.

The audit yields a negative result with a constructive core. Borrowed
metrics fail structurally (\secref{sec:reductio}): perplexity rewards
the flattening it should penalize, structureness mistakes degenerate
repetition for form, several statistics are near-proxies for chart
length, and every chart-only quantity is insensitive to global audio
misalignment. The underlying raw quantities carry signal; what is
missing is calibration and grounding. Normalizing each diagnostic
against per-difficulty target bands estimated from the official corpus
restores a consistent, manipulation-resistant reading
(\secref{sec:layers}), and a timing family anchored on a grid-reference
hierarchy---authored per-bar timing metadata when available, an
audio-estimated beat grid otherwise, never a generated chart's
self-declared grid---closes the misalignment gap without a reference
chart (\secref{sec:timingmethod}).

\textbf{Relation to prior work.} Dance Dance Convolution evaluates step
placement by F-measure against a reference chart and step selection by
LM perplexity~\cite{donahue2017ddc}; later systems inherit this
template~\cite{takada2023genelive,hanzen2025tcp}, and TaikoNation adds
pattern-congruence measures against human
charts~\cite{halina2021taikonation}. None of these metrics has been
audited against controlled damage. In generative-music evaluation, Yang
and Lerch compare feature distributions between generated and reference
sets~\cite{yang2020evaluation}, MusPy popularized symbolic
statistics~\cite{dong2020muspy}, and the Jazz Transformer introduced
self-similarity structureness~\cite{wu2020jazz}; these are set-level or
direction-ambiguous quantities, and we audit their behavior when used
as per-chart quality signals. Theis et al.\ showed that likelihood and
sample quality can diverge~\cite{theis2016note}; our flattening result
is a corpus-LM instance of that dissociation with a concrete attack.
Unlike pure critiques we supply the repair, and unlike human-anchored
metric studies our harness needs no raters. Onset F-measure follows
\texttt{mir\_eval}~\cite{raffel2014mireval}; timing thresholds follow
the psychophysics of temporal
discrimination~\cite{friberg1995jnd,london2012hearing} rather than any
game's scoring windows.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A corruption-based audit harness}: nine dose-controlled
  damage operators with acceptance criteria (population-level monotone
  response, resistance to manipulation, published coupling structure)
  and pre-registered fairness rules, applicable unchanged to any future
  chart metric (\secref{sec:probes}).
\item \textbf{A structural failure analysis} of borrowed metrics: two
  sign-reversal results (perplexity improves under flattening;
  structureness improves under collapse), an insensitivity result
  covering all 56 chart-only quantities under anchor shift, and a
  direction-instability result---perplexity ranks correctly against
  systems noisier than the corpus yet inverts against systems blander
  than it (\secref{sec:reductio}).
\item \textbf{A two-layer, reference-free protocol} with an empirically
  assigned role taxonomy and a perceptually anchored timing family: one
  6\,ms just-noticeable-difference constant grounds a deadzone,
  severity tiers, and a clean-note rate; a piecewise fixed-lattice
  phase detector recovers injected shifts where matching statistics do
  not respond (\secref{sec:layers}--\ref{sec:timingmethod},
  \secref{sec:anchorresults}).
\item \textbf{Complementarity evidence}: three corruptions, each
  undetected by one metric family and strongly detected by another, in
  both directions---the empirical case that chart quality is a profile,
  not a scalar (\secref{sec:complementarity}).
\item \textbf{Application to five published systems} across three chart
  vocabularies, yielding mechanism-level diagnoses and an applicability
  matrix instead of a single ranking (\secref{sec:systemresults}), plus
  released artifacts: an installable package, corruption operators,
  calibration bands, per-chart records, and grouped song-level dataset
  splits. \PENDING{P8: public repo URL.}
\end{enumerate}

The corruptions establish what metrics detect: \emph{damage}. Whether
the surviving metrics also track player experience is a distinct
question that requires perception studies; we return to it in
\secref{sec:discussion}.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Data and grid references}\label{sec:data}

We use a corpus of 1{,}155 taiko-style chart files covering 1{,}013
unique songs, grouped by audio identity and normalized title and split
at the song level into 813/80/120 training/validation/test song groups,
so that no song appears in more than one split. Calibration uses the
official difficulty-course charts of the training songs; evaluation
uses 40 held-out test songs (170 official course-charts) across all
difficulty courses.

Timing is the one family that cannot be measured from the chart alone:
it needs a grid reference. We fix a two-tier hierarchy: (1) the
\emph{authored} grid---per-bar timing metadata (bar lines, per-bar
meter) shipped with the song's official charts, which contains no note
positions and is therefore usable in reference-free evaluation; (2) an
\emph{estimated} grid from an audio beat tracker, for songs without
authored metadata. A generated chart's self-declared grid is never
consulted: a system that supplied its own reference could trivially
zero its own offsets. On our corpus the estimated tier is accurate:
estimated beat periods agree with authored tempo within 2\% on 40/40
test songs.

\subsection{Two-layer scoring}\label{sec:layers}

\textbf{Layer 1: raw diagnostics.} Each chart is a sequence of timed,
typed events. We compute quantities with native units and no claimed
direction: note density and local-density percentiles, inter-onset
entropy, color-switch rate, $n$-gram LM negative log-likelihood
(trigram, add-$\alpha$ smoothing, trained on the calibration corpus),
unseen-transition rate, unique/repeated 4-gram rates, and windowed LM
surprisal percentiles. These deliberately coincide with the borrowed
metrics: the audit compares \emph{normalization strategies}, not
measurement choices.

\textbf{Layer 2: calibrated band scores.} For each diagnostic and
difficulty course we estimate a target band $[p_{10},p_{90}]$ over the
calibration corpus. Scores are $1$ inside the band and decay smoothly
outside; error-type quantities use exponential penalties. Band scores
measure \emph{typicality}: ``more entropy'' is neither good nor bad,
but ``entropy within the range human charters use at this difficulty''
is well defined, and the score is reference-free at the song
level---scoring a chart for a new song requires only the fixed corpus
calibration.

\textbf{Two metric types.} The suite separates \emph{defect-type}
quantities, whose direction is unconditional (transitions that never
occur in the human corpus; violations of playability constraints), from
\emph{typicality-type} quantities, where only the corruption evidence,
not the sign of a deviation, justifies an interpretation. For
typicality scores the inference is one-directional: the corruptions
certify that damage drives charts out of band; they do not certify the
converse. An out-of-band reading is a flag whose meaning is adjudicated
by the rest of the profile (\secref{sec:discussion}).

\textbf{Role taxonomy.} Scores are assigned roles empirically by the
audit, not a priori: \emph{rankers} respond to damage and separate
systems (pattern-IC, transition validity, repetition and variety
adequacy, rhythm complexity); \emph{constraints} saturate near 1 for
all reasonable charts, flag only gross violations (density, strain,
overload, spike), and act as vetoes---they never average into a score;
\emph{diagnostics} carry signal with direction-only interpretation
(the fixed-lattice phase detector; the hypersensitive rare-$n$-gram
rate). We report score profiles, not a single aggregate: fixing
trade-off weights between axes is exactly the judgment we defer to
human data. \tabref{tab:families} summarizes the resulting five-plus-one
metric families.

\subsection{Perceptually anchored timing family}\label{sec:timingmethod}

All timing thresholds are perceptual, not game-specific: a single
6\,ms deadzone---the just-noticeable timing distortion for short
intervals~\cite{friberg1995jnd}---below which deviations are treated as
imperceptible; severity tiers at $1\times/2\times/3\times$ the
deadzone; and a relative channel that compares consecutive inter-onset
intervals against a Weber envelope $\max(6\,\mathrm{ms},\,2.5\%\times
\mathrm{IOI})$, computed on raw offsets before any deadzone is applied.
The family reports a partition of all notes---\emph{clean} (matched
within the deadzone), tier exceedances, and unmatched notes---so that
the primary statistic, the \emph{clean rate}, has unmatched notes in
its denominator and cannot be improved by dropping notes. Aggregation
is per-chart first, never pooled over notes: a chart is judged by its
own worst notes, and cross-chart tails are reported alongside medians.

Global misalignment needs a dedicated detector. A matching-based signed
offset (match each note to its nearest lattice anchor, average the
residuals) re-matches every shifted note on a dense subdivision lattice
and therefore cannot register a constant shift
(\secref{sec:anchorresults}). The \emph{fixed-lattice phase detector}
instead finds the global offset that maximizes support of the note
stream on a lattice laid inside each authored bar (eighth-note, beat,
and bar-line scales combined), searched over $\pm$250\,ms with
minimum-norm tie-breaking. Because the lattice follows the authored bar
lines, it is aperiodic under tempo changes and remains well defined on
songs with meter and tempo variation.

\tabref{tab:probes} lists the operators. Corruption direction is
guaranteed at the population level: because band scores are two-sided,
a corruption can push an individual off-band chart \emph{toward} its
band, so all criteria are evaluated over 170 charts, with per-chart
paired direction (the fraction of charts moving against their own
intact version) as the certification statistic---magnitude medians can
saturate under re-matching effects, paired direction does not.
Acceptance requires (a) \emph{sensitivity}: the designated target
degrades with dose; (b) \emph{resistance to manipulation}: no score
family rewards damage without bound; and (c) \emph{disclosed coupling}:
the full operator$\times$metric response matrix is published rather
than summarized by its diagonal.

Fairness rules fixed in advance: identical corruptions for baselines
and calibrated scores; identity pairs across layers (distinct-4
$\equiv$ unique-4-gram rate; transition validity $\leftrightarrow$
unseen-transition rate) are never counted as independent agreement;
length confounds are checked against note count; and the flattening
attack is additionally run \emph{cross-LM}---driven by an LM trained on
half the calibration songs and measured by an LM trained on all of
them---so that perplexity's failure cannot be an artifact of attacking
its own model.

\subsection{Evaluated systems}\label{sec:systems}

We evaluate five published generators spanning three chart
vocabularies: two taiko-domain systems---%
Mapperatorinator~\cite{mapperatorinator}, an open-source
osu!-ecosystem generator run in taiko mode \PENDING{E: positioning
wording}, and TaikoNation~\cite{halina2021taikonation}---plus, for the
game-agnostic families, Dance Dance Convolution's onset
pipeline~\cite{donahue2017ddc}, the public timing-only configuration of
Gen\'eLive!~\cite{takada2023genelive}, and an osu! difficulty-targeted
generator~\cite{autoosu}. All systems chart the same 40 held-out songs
from audio in their public inference configurations; none was trained
on this corpus. Official charts of the same songs provide the reference
row.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Sensitivity: targeted damage moves the right scores}
\label{sec:sensitivity}

All six operators with calibrated band targets pass: dose--response
Spearman $\rho$(dose, target score) is negative for C1 rhythm
complexity ($-0.48$), C3 transition validity ($-0.33$), C4 repetition
adequacy ($-0.41$), C6 density adequacy ($-0.68$), C7 density spike
($-0.79$), and C8 repetition adequacy ($-0.48$), with monotone mean
trajectories (all $p<10^{-18}$). C2's target is the timing detector
(\secref{sec:anchorresults}); C1s's response pattern is the
complementarity result (\secref{sec:complementarity}); C5's bounded
response is the manipulation-resistance property, reported next.

\subsection{Borrowed metrics reward damage}
\label{sec:reductio}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figs/figA_complementarity.pdf}
\caption{Two corruptions, two blind/detect splits, as per-chart paired
net direction (fraction of charts moving up minus moving down relative
to their own intact version). \textbf{Left (C5 flattening):} rewriting
notes toward the LM argmax drives $n$-gram NLL \emph{down} in
essentially every chart (net direction $-1.0$ at full dose)---an
improvement under the standard lower-is-better reading---while the band
score built on the same LM statistic stays near zero net direction: it
saturates at typicality instead of rewarding the damage. \textbf{Right
(C1s sparse outliers):} displacing 0.5--2\% of notes by $\pm$60\,ms
leaves mean timing summaries at exactly zero median response (dashed),
moves the timing clean rate in only a fraction of charts (the
subdivision lattice re-matches $\approx$70\% of displaced notes within
the deadzone), but flips the IOI token class of adjacent intervals, so
grammar NLL responds with net direction $+0.86$ even at one to three
displaced notes per chart. Timing damage that placement metrics absorb
is detected as grammar damage.}
\label{fig:complementarity}
\end{figure}

\figref{fig:complementarity} (left) and two corpus numbers give the
sign-reversal results. \emph{Perplexity rewards flattening}: official
test charts average perplexity 9.4 under the corpus trigram; rewriting
them toward the LM's modal patterns lowers perplexity by 37\% (9.44
$\to$ 5.98). Read as lower-is-better~\cite{donahue2017ddc}, perplexity
prefers the damaged chart---a corpus-LM instance of the
likelihood/quality dissociation~\cite{theis2016note}---and the reversal
survives the cross-LM version of the attack (9.44 $\to$ 6.04,
$-$36\%). The band score over the
same statistic cannot be driven above typicality (bounded at 1), and
the damage the attack actually does is registered where it lands:
repetition adequacy falls from 0.99 to 0.84. \emph{Structureness
rewards collapse}: replacing charts with their own dominant 4-gram loop
raises self-similarity structureness~\cite{wu2020jazz} by 62\% (0.085
$\to$ 0.137)---copy-paste output is maximally self-similar---and
set-level Self-BLEU rises from 0.845 to 0.898, while repetition and
variety adequacy fall (0.99 $\to$ 0.80 and 0.99 $\to$ 0.89).

Two observational results complete the case. \emph{Direction
instability}: against the two published taiko generators of
\secref{sec:systemresults}, whose outputs are noisier than the human
corpus, perplexity separates official from generated charts in the
correct direction (AUC 0.80)---yet C5 proves the same reading inverts
for outputs blander than the corpus. A signed quality metric whose sign
depends on which side of the human distribution the system sits cannot
be used for ranking; band scores are stable on both sides by
construction. \emph{Length confounds}: on the same pool, type--token
ratio correlates with note count at $|\rho|=0.85$ and distinct-2 at
$0.56$, against at most $0.28$ for the calibrated rankers.

\subsection{Anchor shift: 56 insensitive metrics, one detector}
\label{sec:anchorresults}

A chart can be idiomatic on every chart-only axis and still be
displaced from the music entirely---C2 makes this concrete. A constant
$+15/30/60$\,ms shift of every note changes all 56 chart-only
quantities by at most 0.03 standard deviations, and the matching-based
signed offset does not respond either, for the reason given in
\secref{sec:timingmethod}: a dense subdivision lattice re-matches every
shifted note. The fixed-lattice phase detector recovers the injected
shifts with exact medians (15.00/30.00/60.00\,ms) on at least 99.4\% of
charts on \emph{both} grid tiers. Under dense jitter (C1) the rest of
the timing family also certifies cleanly: the clean rate falls in
100\% of charts at every dose, and the first-tier exceedance at
$\pm$10\,ms jitter matches its theoretical value
($P(|U(-10,10)|>6)=0.4$; measured median 0.39).

\subsection{Complementarity: the profile cannot be reduced}
\label{sec:complementarity}

Three corruptions produce three different detection splits
(\figref{fig:complementarity}, \figref{fig:couplingmap}):

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C2 anchor shift}: registered by none of the 56
  chart-only quantities and not by matching-based offsets; detected
  only by the timing-family phase detector.
\item \textbf{C1s sparse outliers}: largely absorbed by lattice
  matching ($\approx$70\% of displaced notes re-match within the
  deadzone; mean summaries show exactly zero median response); detected
  by the grammar family (net direction $+0.86$ to $+0.88$), because a
  60\,ms displacement necessarily flips the IOI token class of two
  adjacent intervals. Density and structure do not respond ($|$net
  direction$|\le 0.02$)---the response is specific, only not where the
  operator was aimed.
\item \textbf{C5 flattening}: rewards the grammar baseline
  (perplexity), is bounded by the band layer, and is detected by the
  structure family.
\end{itemize}

Each family has a corruption it cannot register and a corruption only
it registers. Any scalar aggregate would have to fix exchange rates
between axes that these results show are not exchangeable.

\figref{fig:tiers} and \figref{fig:profiles} give the profiles of the
five systems of \secref{sec:systems}; three findings illustrate what
the profile adds over single numbers.

\textbf{Equal scores, different failure mechanisms.} The two taiko
systems have nearly equal timing clean rates (0.59 vs.\ 0.58), yet
opposite mechanisms. Mapperatorinator is internally coherent
(relative-channel violations 0.10, close to officials) but
phase-misanchored: the phase detector reads a 26\,ms median offset,
while the matching-based statistic reads under 1\,ms---the same
re-matching failure that C2 demonstrated, measured on a published
system. TaikoNation deviates on both channels (relative violations
0.41; phase offset 55\,ms) and, uniquely among the five, shows no
relation to the audio's dynamics: its density--energy correlation is
$-0.003$ and its notes land on average-energy frames (at-onset envelope
$z=-0.004$, against 1.09 for officials). The onset pipeline anchors
accurately (2.5\,ms), which doubles as a control: all five systems
charted the same audio files, so the 26--55\,ms offsets are model
behavior, not pipeline artifacts.

\textbf{Complementary failures.} On chart-vocabulary families the two
taiko systems fail on disjoint axes (\figref{fig:profiles}).
Mapperatorinator writes in-band pattern language (repeated-4-gram rate
0.51 against a human band of 0.45--0.72; repetition and variety
adequacy 1.0) but fails difficulty conditioning: its oni and ura
courses sit inside their density bands while its easy/normal/hard
courses run $1.5$--$2.5\times$ over theirs (easy: 2.9 notes/s against
a band of 0.8--1.8). Its ``easy'' does not match the density of a
taiko easy course---a failure only per-difficulty calibration can
register, and one that trips the playability gate. TaikoNation shows
the reverse pattern: density and strain in band, but pattern language
far outside the corpus (perplexity 161 against 9.4 for officials; 55\%
of transitions never occur in the human corpus; repeated-4-gram rate
0.004, i.e.\ no learnable repetition).

\textbf{Progress statements need profiles.} Between TaikoNation (2021)
and Mapperatorinator (2024) the timing clean rate is unchanged---a
single-number comparison would report three years without progress.
The profile shows where the progress went: sequence coherence improved
by a factor of four (relative violations 0.41 $\to$ 0.10) and pattern
language moved from far outside the corpus to in-band, while anchoring
and difficulty calibration remain open problems.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Out-of-band is a flag, not a verdict.} The corruptions license
a one-directional inference: damage drives charts out of band. The
converse is not certified, and should not be---a generator that
produces charts outside the human envelope while passing the
constraint gates, coupling to the music, and retaining learnable
structure is a candidate for originality, not a failure. In the
novelty-plus-value framing of creativity, the typicality axes measure
the magnitude of novelty while the defect-type axes and the rest of
the profile supply the value judgment; the same out-of-band reading
means damage when the value axes collapse and material for human
review when they hold. This adjudication is itself an argument for
profiles: the meaning of one axis depends on the others.

\textbf{Beyond one game.} Two families read only note times and were
applied unchanged across three chart vocabularies here (timing,
coupling); the token families (grammar, structure, gap) transfer with
a vocabulary adapter---lane, position-bin, or direction tokens replace
color tokens---plus a calibration corpus of human charts for the
target game, and the constraint family re-calibrates its limits from
the same corpus. The corruption operators act on $(t,\mathrm{class})$
event streams and port as-is, so the certification protocol, not any
single metric, is the transferable artifact: given a new game's
corpus, the same harness determines which metrics work there.
Position-based games additionally need a spatial-ergonomics family
(movement cost, hand allocation, rotation) that no vocabulary
adaptation of the present families supplies; we leave it as the main
structural extension.

\textbf{Limitations.} The corruptions certify damage detection, not
experience prediction; validating the surviving metrics against player
judgments---including the novelty-vs-damage adjudication above---
remains future work. Direction is guaranteed at the population level:
a minority of individual charts move toward their band under
corruption, so per-chart verdicts need the profile, not one score. The
suite is a coupled profile (\figref{fig:couplingmap}); attribution
between timing and pattern damage is shared through tokenization,
which is partly a property of the domain (mistimed notes damage
patterns as played). One game corpus calibrates the bands; the
cross-game systems are evaluated on the game-agnostic families only;
systems are run in their public inference configurations at one seed.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Chart generation needs evaluation it can trust more than it needs
another metric. Corruption-based validation is systematic and
reproducible, and it showed that the borrowed metrics did not merely
underperform: they rewarded flattening, rewarded collapse, missed a
global misalignment ten times the perceptual threshold, and inverted
ranking direction across system regimes. Corpus-calibrated band scores
over the same raw quantities, extended with a perceptually anchored
timing family over a grid-reference hierarchy, pass the same
corruptions---population-exact dose response, bounded response to the
manipulation attack, exact shift recovery---and their coupling
structure is published rather than hidden. Applied to five published
systems, the resulting profiles replace rankings with mechanism-level
diagnoses: systems with equal scores and disjoint failure axes,
progress that single numbers cannot register, and
difficulty-conditioning failures that only per-difficulty calibration
detects. The harness, calibration artifacts, per-chart records, and
evaluation package are released so that future chart-quality
metrics---for this game or others---can be validated under the same
protocol. \PENDING{P8: artifact URL.}
