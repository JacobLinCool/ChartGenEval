\begin{abstract}
Rhythm-game charts are designed artifacts with many valid realizations
per song, yet generated charts are still evaluated with metrics borrowed
from other tasks: F-measure against one reference chart, language-model
(LM) perplexity, and unreferenced statistics such as entropy,
distinct-$n$, and self-similarity. We validate metrics the way one would
test a smoke detector: by setting controlled fires. Applying nine
targeted corruption probes at increasing doses to 170 human charts
(4{,}760 scored variants), we find failures invisible to spot checks:
perplexity \emph{improves} by 37\% when charts are flattened toward the
LM's modal patterns, self-similarity structureness \emph{rises} by 62\%
under copy-paste collapse, and all 56 chart-only quantities we compute
are blind to a global 60\,ms audio misalignment that would be fatal in
play (max response 0.03 SD). A two-layer protocol repairs these
failures: raw diagnostics are normalized against per-difficulty target
bands calibrated on the official-chart corpus, and a perceptually
anchored timing family---a 6\,ms just-noticeable-difference deadzone,
severity tiers at its multiples, and a fixed-lattice phase witness over
a two-tier grid-reference hierarchy---recovers injected 15/30/60\,ms
shifts with exact medians on $\ge$99.4\% of charts, with no reference
chart. The probes also expose complementarity: a sparse-outlier
corruption that grid matching largely absorbs is caught at the token
level by the grammar family, while the anchor shift invisible to every
chart-only metric is caught only by the timing witness---the score
profile is not compressible to a scalar. Applied to five published
generators across three chart vocabularies, the profile turns
single-number rankings into mechanism diagnoses. We release the probes,
calibration artifacts, per-chart records, and an installable evaluation
package. \PENDING{P8: artifact URL.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Automatic chart generation for rhythm games---mapping audio to a
playable sequence of timed, typed notes---has progressed from
onset-detection pipelines to conditional sequence
models~\cite{donahue2017ddc,halina2021taikonation,takada2023genelive,
hanzen2025tcp}. Evaluation has not kept pace. Because a chart is a
creative artifact rather than an annotation, the same song admits many
valid charts, and the field's dominant tool---F-measure against the
single official chart---penalizes every valid alternative equally and
cannot be computed at all for songs without charts. The natural
complement is reference-free measurement, for which authors borrow from
symbolic music and text generation: LM perplexity~\cite{donahue2017ddc},
pattern-space congruence~\cite{halina2021taikonation}, event entropy,
distinct-$n$~\cite{li2016diversity}, Self-BLEU~\cite{zhu2018texygen},
feature-distribution comparison~\cite{yang2020evaluation,dong2020muspy},
and self-similarity structureness~\cite{wu2020jazz}.

This paper asks whether those borrowed metrics measure chart quality at
all, and answers with a validation method that requires no listening
test: \emph{targeted corruption}. Starting from human (official) charts,
we apply nine damage operators---dense timing jitter, sparse severe
outliers, global anchor shift, color shuffling, copy-paste collapse,
LM-argmax blandification, density scaling, burst insertion, bar
shuffling---at three doses each. On average, corrupting a human chart
cannot make it better, so a usable quality metric must respond in the
right direction, and the response must grow with dose. Metric validation
thus becomes a fully automated, reproducible procedure that requires
neither human raters nor model training, and applies unchanged to any
proposed chart metric.

The audit yields a negative result with a constructive core. Borrowed
metrics fail structurally (\secref{sec:reductio}): perplexity rewards
the blandness it should punish, structureness mistakes degenerate
repetition for form, several statistics are near-proxies for chart
length, and every chart-only quantity is blind to global audio
misalignment. But the underlying raw quantities carry signal; what is
missing is calibration and grounding. We show that (i) normalizing each
diagnostic against per-difficulty target bands estimated from the
official corpus restores a consistent, gaming-resistant reading, and
(ii) a timing family anchored on a grid-reference hierarchy---authored
per-bar timing metadata when available, an audio-estimated beat grid
otherwise, never a generated chart's self-declared grid---closes the
misalignment blind spot with no reference chart.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A corruption-probe audit harness}: nine dose-controlled
  damage operators with acceptance criteria (population-level monotone
  response, anti-gaming, published coupling structure) and fairness
  rules, applicable unchanged to any future chart metric
  (\secref{sec:probes}).
\item \textbf{A structural failure analysis} of borrowed metrics: two
  reductio exhibits (perplexity improves under blandification;
  structureness improves under collapse), an all-blind result under
  anchor shift, and a direction-instability result---perplexity ranks
  correctly against noisier-than-corpus systems yet inverts against
  blander-than-corpus ones (\secref{sec:results}).
\item \textbf{A two-layer, reference-free protocol} with an empirically
  assigned role taxonomy and a perceptually anchored timing family: a
  single 6\,ms JND constant grounds a deadzone, severity tiers, and a
  clean-note rate; a piecewise fixed-lattice witness recovers injected
  shifts exactly where matching statistics stay blind
  (\secref{sec:protocol}, \secref{sec:timing}).
\item \textbf{Complementarity evidence}: three corruptions, each
  invisible to one metric family and glaring to another, in both
  directions---the empirical case that chart quality is a profile, not
  a scalar (\secref{sec:complementarity}).
\item \textbf{Application to five published systems} across three chart
  vocabularies, yielding mechanism diagnoses and an applicability
  matrix instead of a single ranking (\secref{sec:application}), plus
  released artifacts: an installable package, probe implementations,
  calibration bands, per-chart records, and grouped song-level dataset
  splits. \PENDING{P8: public repo URL.}
\end{enumerate}

The probes establish what metrics detect: \emph{damage}. Whether the
surviving metrics also track player experience is a distinct question
that requires perception studies; we return to it in
\secref{sec:limits}.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Chart generation and its evaluation.} Dance Dance Convolution
evaluates step placement by F-measure against a reference chart and step
selection by LM perplexity~\cite{donahue2017ddc}; later systems inherit
this template~\cite{takada2023genelive,hanzen2025tcp}. TaikoNation adds
pattern-congruence measures against human
charts~\cite{halina2021taikonation}. None of these metrics has been
audited against controlled damage.

\textbf{Generative-music evaluation.} Yang and Lerch compare feature
distributions between generated and reference
sets~\cite{yang2020evaluation}; MusPy popularized symbolic
statistics~\cite{dong2020muspy}; the Jazz Transformer introduced
self-similarity structureness indicators~\cite{wu2020jazz}. These are
set-level or direction-ambiguous quantities; we audit their behavior
when used as per-chart quality signals.

\textbf{Metric critiques.} Theis et al.\ showed that likelihood and
sample quality can diverge arbitrarily~\cite{theis2016note}; our
blandification result is a sharp corpus-LM instance. NLG research
documented pathologies of distinct-$n$ and
Self-BLEU~\cite{li2016diversity,zhu2018texygen}. Unlike pure critiques
we supply the repair, and unlike human-anchored metric studies our
harness needs no raters. Onset F-measure follows
\texttt{mir\_eval}~\cite{raffel2014mireval}. Timing thresholds follow
the psychophysics of temporal discrimination~\cite{friberg1995jnd,
london2012hearing} rather than any game's scoring windows.

\section{Two-Layer Evaluation Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

\textbf{Data.} We use a corpus of 1{,}155 taiko-style chart files
covering 1{,}013 unique songs, grouped by audio identity and normalized
title and split at the song level into 813/80/120
training/validation/test song groups, so that no song appears in more
than one split. Calibration uses the official difficulty-course charts
of the training songs; evaluation uses 40 held-out test songs (170
official course-charts) across all difficulty courses.

\textbf{Layer 1: raw diagnostics.} Each chart is a sequence of timed,
typed events. We compute quantities with native units and no claimed
direction: note density and local-density percentiles, inter-onset
entropy, color-switch rate, $n$-gram LM negative log-likelihood
(trigram, add-$\alpha$ smoothing, trained on the calibration corpus),
unseen-transition rate, unique/repeated 4-gram rates, and windowed LM
surprisal percentiles. These deliberately coincide with the borrowed
metrics: the audit compares \emph{normalization strategies}, not
measurement choices.

\textbf{Layer 2: calibrated band scores.} For each diagnostic and
difficulty course we estimate a target band $[p_{10},p_{90}]$ over the
calibration corpus. Scores are $1$ inside the band and decay smoothly
outside; error-type quantities use exponential penalties. Band scores
measure \emph{typicality}: ``more entropy'' is neither good nor bad,
but ``entropy within the range human charters use at this difficulty''
is well defined, and the score is reference-free at the song
level---scoring a chart for a novel song requires only the fixed corpus
calibration.

\textbf{Two metric types.} The suite separates \emph{defect-type}
quantities, whose direction is unconditional (transitions that never
occur in the human corpus; violations of playability constraints), from
\emph{typicality-type} quantities, where only the probes---not the
sign of the deviation---justify an interpretation. For typicality
scores the inference is one-directional: the probes certify that damage
drives charts out of band; they do not certify the converse. An
out-of-band reading is a flag whose meaning is adjudicated by the rest
of the profile (\secref{sec:discussion}).

\textbf{Role taxonomy.} Scores are assigned roles empirically by the
audit, not a priori: \emph{rankers} respond to damage and separate
systems (pattern-IC, transition validity, repetition and variety
adequacy, rhythm complexity); \emph{constraints} saturate near 1 for
all reasonable charts, flag only gross violations (density, strain,
overload, spike), and act as vetoes---they never average into a score;
\emph{diagnostics} carry signal with direction-only interpretation
(the fixed-lattice phase witness; the hypersensitive rare-$n$-gram
rate). We report score profiles, not a single aggregate: fixing
trade-off weights between axes is exactly the judgment we defer to
human data.

\tabref{tab:probes} lists the probes. Corruption direction is
guaranteed at the population level: since band scores are two-sided, a
corruption can push an individual off-band chart \emph{toward} its band,
so all criteria are evaluated over 170 charts, with per-chart paired
direction (the fraction of charts moving against their own intact
version) as the certification statistic---magnitude medians can
saturate under re-matching effects, paired direction does not.
Acceptance requires (a) \emph{sensitivity}: the designated target
degrades with dose; (b) \emph{anti-gaming}: no score family rewards
damage without bound; and (c) \emph{disclosed coupling}: the full
probe$\times$metric response matrix is published rather than summarized
by its diagonal.

Fairness rules fixed in advance: identical probes for baselines and
calibrated scores; identity pairs across layers (distinct-4 $\equiv$
unique-4-gram rate; transition validity $\leftrightarrow$
unseen-transition rate) are never counted as independent agreement;
length confounds are checked against note count; and the
blandification attack is additionally run \emph{cross-LM}: the attack
is driven by an LM trained on half the calibration songs and measured
by an LM trained on all of them, so perplexity's failure cannot be an
artifact of attacking its own model.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Sensitivity: targeted damage moves the right scores}
\label{sec:sensitivity}

All six probes with calibrated band targets pass: dose--response
Spearman $\rho$(dose, target score) is negative for C1 rhythm
complexity ($-0.48$), C3 transition validity ($-0.33$), C4 repetition
adequacy ($-0.41$), C6 density adequacy ($-0.68$), C7 density spike
($-0.79$), and C8 repetition adequacy ($-0.48$), with monotone mean
trajectories (all $p<10^{-18}$). C2's target is the timing witness
(\secref{sec:timing}); C1s's response pattern is the complementarity
exhibit (\secref{sec:complementarity}); C5's bounded response is the
anti-gaming property, reported next.

\subsection{Reductio: borrowed metrics reward damage}
\label{sec:reductio}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering

\caption{Bidirectional blindness, as per-chart paired net direction

(net direction $-1.0$ at full dose)---``better'' under the standard

\label{fig:complementarity}

\figref{fig:complementarity} (left) and two corpus numbers give the
reductio exhibits. \emph{Perplexity rewards blandness}: official test
charts average perplexity 9.4 under the corpus trigram; flattening them
toward the LM's modal patterns drives perplexity down 37\% (9.44
$\to$ 5.98). Read as ``lower is better''~\cite{donahue2017ddc},
perplexity prefers the damaged chart---a corpus-LM instance of the
likelihood/quality dissociation~\cite{theis2016note}---and the reversal
survives the cross-LM version of the attack.
The band score over the same statistic cannot be driven above
typicality (bounded at 1), and the damage the attack actually does is
caught where it lands: repetition adequacy falls 0.99 $\to$ 0.84.
\emph{Structureness rewards collapse}: replacing charts with their own
dominant 4-gram loop raises self-similarity
structureness~\cite{wu2020jazz} by 62\% (0.085 $\to$ 0.137)---copy-paste
is maximally self-similar---and set-level Self-BLEU rises in lockstep
(0.845 $\to$ 0.898), while repetition and variety adequacy correctly
fall (0.99 $\to$ 0.80, 0.99 $\to$ 0.89).

Two observational results complete the case. \emph{Direction
instability}: against the two published taiko generators of
\secref{sec:application}, whose outputs are noisier than the human
corpus, perplexity separates official from generated charts in the
correct direction (AUC 0.80)---yet C5 proves the same reading inverts
for outputs blander than the corpus. A signed quality metric whose sign
depends on which side of the human distribution the system happens to
sit is not usable as a ranking signal; band scores are stable on both
sides by construction. \emph{Length confounds}: on the same pool,
type--token ratio correlates with note count at $|\rho|=0.85$ and
distinct-2 at $0.56$, against $\le 0.28$ for every calibrated ranker.

\subsection{A perceptually anchored timing family}
\label{sec:timing}

Timing is the one family that cannot be measured from the chart alone:
it needs a grid reference. We fix a two-tier hierarchy: (1) the
\emph{authored} grid---per-bar timing metadata (bar lines, per-bar
meter) shipped with the song's official charts, which carries no note
positions and is therefore fair for reference-free evaluation; (2) an
\emph{estimated} grid from an audio beat tracker, for songs without
authored metadata. A generated chart's self-declared grid is never
consulted: a system that supplied its own reference could trivially
zero its own offsets. On our corpus the estimated tier costs little:
estimated beat periods agree with authored tempo within 2\% on 40/40
test songs, and phase-sensitive statistics remain usable through the
witness below.

All thresholds are perceptual, not game-specific: a single 6\,ms
deadzone---the just-noticeable timing distortion for short
intervals~\cite{friberg1995jnd}---below which deviations are treated as
imperceptible; severity tiers at $1\times/2\times/3\times$ the
deadzone; and a relative channel that compares consecutive inter-onset
intervals against a Weber envelope $\max(6\,\mathrm{ms},\,2.5\%\times
\mathrm{IOI})$, computed on raw offsets before any deadzone is applied.
The family reports a partition of all notes---\emph{clean} (matched
within the deadzone), tier exceedances, and unmatched notes---so that
the primary statistic, the \emph{clean rate}, has unmatched notes in
its denominator and cannot be improved by losing notes. Aggregation is
per-chart first, never pooled over notes: a chart is ruined by its own
worst notes, and cross-chart tails are reported alongside medians.

Under dense jitter (C1) the family certifies cleanly: the clean rate
falls in 100\% of charts at every dose, and the first-tier exceedance
at $\pm$10\,ms jitter matches its theoretical value
($P(|U(-10,10)|>6)=0.4$; measured median 0.39).

\textbf{Closing the anchor blind spot.} A chart can be idiomatic on
every chart-only axis and still be shifted off the music entirely---C2
makes this concrete. A constant $+15/30/60$\,ms shift of every note
changes all 56 chart-only quantities by at most 0.03 SD, and the
obvious fix is equally blind: a matching-based signed offset (match
each note to its nearest lattice anchor, average the residuals)
re-matches every shifted note on a dense subdivision lattice. Detecting
the shift requires a \emph{fixed}-lattice witness: the global offset
that maximizes support of the note stream on a lattice laid inside each
authored bar (eighth-note, beat, and bar-line scales combined), searched
over $\pm$250\,ms with minimum-norm tie-breaking. Because the lattice
follows the authored bar lines it is aperiodic under tempo changes, and
the witness recovers the injected shifts with exact medians
(15.00/30.00/60.00\,ms) on $\ge$99.4\% of charts on \emph{both} grid
tiers. The same witness applied to published systems is what detects
their anchoring behavior in \secref{sec:application}.

\subsection{Complementarity: the profile is not compressible}
\label{sec:complementarity}

Three probes, three different blind/catch splits
(\figref{fig:complementarity}, \figref{fig:couplingmap}):

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C2 anchor shift}: invisible to all 56 chart-only
  quantities and to matching-based offsets; caught only by the timing
  witness.
\item \textbf{C1s sparse outliers}: largely absorbed by lattice
  matching ($\approx$70\% of displaced notes re-match within the
  deadzone; mean summaries show exactly zero median response); caught
  by the grammar family (net direction $+0.86$ to $+0.88$), because a
  60\,ms displacement necessarily flips the IOI token class of two
  adjacent intervals. Density and structure stay silent ($|$net
  direction$|\le 0.02$)---the response is specific, just not where the
  probe was aimed.
\item \textbf{C5 blandification}: fools the grammar baseline
  (perplexity), is bounded by the band layer, and is caught by the
  structure family.
\end{itemize}

Each family has a corruption it cannot see and a corruption only it
sees. Any scalar aggregate would have to fix exchange rates between
axes that these results show are not exchangeable.

We evaluate five published generators spanning three chart
vocabularies: two taiko-domain systems---Mapperatorinator~\cite{mapperatorinator}, an
open-source osu!-ecosystem generator run in taiko mode
\PENDING{E: positioning wording}, and
TaikoNation~\cite{halina2021taikonation}---plus, for the game-agnostic
families, Dance Dance Convolution's onset
pipeline~\cite{donahue2017ddc}, the public timing-only configuration of
Gen\'eLive!~\cite{takada2023genelive}, and an osu! difficulty-targeted
generator~\cite{autoosu}. All systems chart the same 40 held-out songs from audio;
none was trained on this corpus. \figref{fig:tiers} and
\figref{fig:profiles} give the profiles; three findings illustrate what
they add over single numbers.

\textbf{Same score, different diseases.} The two taiko systems have
statistically indistinguishable timing clean rates (0.59 vs.\ 0.58),
yet opposite mechanisms. Mapperatorinator is internally coherent
(relative-channel violations 0.10, close to officials) but
phase-misanchored: the witness reads a 26\,ms median offset, while the
matching-based statistic reads under 1\,ms---the C2 lesson live on a
published system. TaikoNation is incoherent on both channels (relative
violations 0.41; witness 55\,ms) and, uniquely among the five, deaf to
the music: its density--energy correlation is $-0.003$ and its notes
land on average-energy frames (at-hit envelope $z=-0.004$, vs.\ 1.09
for officials). The onset pipeline anchors accurately (2.5\,ms), which
doubles as a control: all five systems charted the same audio files, so
the 26--55\,ms offsets are model behavior, not pipeline artifacts.

\textbf{Complementary failures, invisible to any scalar.}
On chart-vocabulary families the two taiko systems are near-perfect
complements (\figref{fig:profiles}). Mapperatorinator writes in-band
pattern language (repeated-4-gram rate 0.51 against a human band of
0.45--0.72; repetition and variety adequacy 1.0) but fails difficulty
conditioning: its oni and ura courses sit inside their density bands
while its easy/normal/hard courses run $1.5$--$2.5\times$ over theirs
(easy: 2.9 notes/s against a band of 0.8--1.8)---its ``easy'' is not a
taiko easy, a failure only per-difficulty calibration can see, and one
that trips the playability gate. TaikoNation is the mirror image:
density and strain in band, but pattern language collapsed (perplexity
161 vs.\ 9.4 for officials; 55\% of transitions never occur in the
human corpus; repeated-4-gram rate 0.004---no learnable repetition at
all).

\textbf{Progress statements need profiles.} Between TaikoNation (2021)
and Mapperatorinator (2024) the timing clean rate is unchanged---a
single-number comparison would report three years of no progress. The
profile shows where the progress went: sequence coherence improved by
a factor of four (relative violations 0.41 $\to$ 0.10) and pattern
language moved from collapsed to in-band, while anchoring and
difficulty calibration remain open problems.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Out-of-band is a flag, not a verdict.} The probes license a
one-directional inference: damage drives charts out of band. The
converse is not certified, and should not be---a generator that
produces charts outside the human envelope while passing the
constraint gates, coupling to the music, and retaining learnable
structure is a candidate for novelty, not a failure. In the creativity
framing of novelty-plus-value, the typicality axes measure novelty's
magnitude while the defect-type axes and the rest of the profile
supply the value judgment; the same manifold-distance reading means
``damage'' when the value axes collapse and ``originality to review''
when they hold. This adjudication is itself an argument for profiles:
the meaning of one axis depends on the others.

\textbf{Beyond one game.} Two families read only note times and were
applied unchanged across three chart vocabularies here (timing,
coupling); the token families (grammar, structure, gap) transfer with
a vocabulary adapter---lane, position-bin, or direction tokens replace
color tokens---plus a calibration corpus of human charts for the target
game, and the constraint family re-calibrates its limits from the same
corpus. The probes themselves operate on $(t,\mathrm{class})$ event
streams and port as-is, so the certification protocol, not any single
metric, is the transferable artifact: given a new game's corpus, the
same harness certifies which metrics work there. Position-based games
(4K, osu!, maimai) additionally need a spatial-ergonomics family
(movement cost, hand allocation, rotation) that no chart-vocabulary
adaptation of the present families supplies; we leave it as the main
structural extension.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{No human validation.} The probes certify damage detection, not
experience prediction; validating the surviving metrics against player
judgments---including the novelty-vs-damage adjudication of
\secref{sec:discussion}---remains future work.
\textbf{Population-level direction.} A minority of individual charts
move toward their band under corruption; per-chart verdicts need the
profile, not one score.
\textbf{Coupling.} The suite is a coupled profile
(\figref{fig:couplingmap}); attribution between timing and pattern
damage is smeared by shared tokenization, which is partly domain truth
(mistimed notes damage patterns as played).
\textbf{Scope.} One game corpus calibrates the bands; the cross-game
systems are evaluated on the game-agnostic families only; systems are
run in their public inference configurations at one seed.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Chart generation needs evaluation it can trust more than it needs
another metric. Corruption probes make metric validation systematic and
reproducible: the borrowed metrics did not merely underperform---they
rewarded blandness, rewarded collapse, missed a fatal global
misalignment entirely, and flipped ranking direction across system
regimes. Corpus-calibrated band scores over the same raw quantities,
extended with a perceptually anchored timing family over a grid
reference hierarchy, pass the same probes---population-exact dose
response, bounded response to the gaming attack, exact shift
recovery---and their coupling structure is published rather than
hidden. Applied to five published systems, the resulting profiles
replace rankings with mechanism diagnoses: same-score systems with
opposite diseases, progress that single numbers cannot see, and
difficulty-conditioning failures only per-difficulty calibration
detects. The harness, calibration artifacts, per-chart records, and
evaluation package are released so that future chart-quality
metrics---for this game or others---can be validated under the same
protocol. \PENDING{P8: artifact URL.}

\section{Ethics Statement}
The corpus consists of community-created charts used under research
terms; no personal data is involved. Better automated evaluation
chiefly benefits players and small developers; we see no direct misuse
vector beyond automated content farming, which platform moderation
already addresses.
