\begin{abstract}
Rhythm-game charts are designed artifacts with many valid realizations per
song, yet generated charts are still evaluated with metrics borrowed from
other tasks: F-measure against one reference chart, corpus language-model
(LM) perplexity, and unreferenced statistics such as entropy,
distinct-$n$, and self-similarity. We validate metrics the way one would
test a smoke detector: by setting controlled fires. Applying eight
targeted corruption probes at increasing doses to 167 human charts
(4{,}175 scored variants), we find that borrowed metrics fail in ways
invisible to spot checks: $n$-gram perplexity \emph{improves} by 31\%
when charts are flattened toward the LM's modal patterns, self-similarity
structureness \emph{rises} by 50\% under copy-paste collapse, and every
chart-only metric we tested (35 of 35) is completely blind to a global
60\,ms audio misalignment that would be fatal in play. A two-layer
protocol---raw diagnostics normalized against per-difficulty target bands
calibrated on the official-chart corpus, plus an audio-anchored timing
family whose anchors come from an estimated beat grid---repairs these
failures: six of six targeted band scores degrade monotonically with dose
(Spearman $\rho$ $-0.33$ to $-0.80$), the band scores resist the attack
that fools perplexity, and the fixed-grid timing metric recovers injected
15/30/60\,ms shifts to within 0.2\,ms without any reference chart. We
release the probes, calibration artifacts, per-chart records, and an
installable evaluation package.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Automatic chart generation for rhythm games---mapping audio to a playable
sequence of timed, typed notes---has progressed from onset-detection
pipelines to conditional sequence
models~\cite{donahue2017ddc,halina2021taikonation,takada2023genelive,
hanzen2025tcp}. Evaluation has not kept pace. Because a chart is a
creative artifact rather than an annotation, the same song admits many
valid charts, and the field's dominant tool---F-measure against the
single official chart---penalizes every valid alternative equally and
cannot be computed at all for songs without charts. The natural
complement is reference-free measurement, for which authors borrow from
symbolic music and text generation: LM perplexity~\cite{donahue2017ddc},
pattern-space congruence~\cite{halina2021taikonation}, event entropy,
distinct-$n$~\cite{li2016diversity}, Self-BLEU~\cite{zhu2018texygen},
feature-distribution comparison~\cite{yang2020evaluation,dong2020muspy},
and self-similarity structureness~\cite{wu2020jazz}.

This paper asks whether those borrowed metrics measure chart quality at
all, and answers with a validation method that requires no listening
test: \emph{targeted corruption}. Starting from human (official) charts,
we apply eight damage operators---timing jitter, global anchor shift,
color shuffling, copy-paste collapse, LM-argmax blandification, density
scaling, burst insertion, bar shuffling---at three doses each. On
average, corrupting a human chart cannot make it better, so a usable
quality metric must respond in the right direction, and the response must
grow with dose. Metric validation thus becomes a fully automated,
reproducible procedure that requires neither human raters nor model
training, and applies unchanged to any proposed chart metric.

The audit yields a negative result with a constructive core. Borrowed
metrics fail structurally (\secref{sec:reductio}): perplexity rewards the
blandness it should punish, structureness mistakes degenerate repetition
for form, several statistics are near-proxies for chart length, and all
chart-only metrics are blind to global audio misalignment. But the
underlying raw quantities carry signal; what is missing is calibration
and grounding. We show that (i) normalizing each diagnostic against
per-difficulty target bands estimated from the official corpus restores a
consistent ``better'' direction and resists gaming, and (ii) anchoring a
timing family on a beat grid \emph{estimated from audio} closes the
misalignment blind spot with no reference chart and no measurable
accuracy cost.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A corruption-probe audit harness} for chart-quality
  metrics: eight dose-controlled damage operators with acceptance
  criteria (monotone dose response; anti-gaming; published coupling
  structure) and fairness rules, applicable to any future metric
  (\secref{sec:probes}).
\item \textbf{A structural failure analysis} of 14 borrowed metrics,
  including two reductio exhibits---perplexity improves under
  blandification; structureness improves under collapse---and an
  all-blind result for chart-only metrics under anchor shift
  (\secref{sec:results}).
\item \textbf{A two-layer, reference-free evaluation protocol}: raw
  diagnostics plus corpus-calibrated band scores with an empirically
  assigned role taxonomy, extended with an audio-anchored timing family
  whose fixed-grid offset detector recovers injected shifts to sub-ms
  accuracy (\secref{sec:protocol}, \secref{sec:timing}).
\item \textbf{Released artifacts}: an installable evaluation package,
  probe implementations, calibration bands, all per-chart records, and
  the grouped song-level dataset splits. \PENDING{P8: public repo URL.}
\end{enumerate}

The probes establish what metrics detect: \emph{damage}. Whether the
surviving metrics also track player experience is a distinct question
that requires perception studies; we address it in
\secref{sec:limits}.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Chart generation and its evaluation.} Dance Dance Convolution
evaluates step placement by F-measure against a reference chart and step
selection by LM perplexity~\cite{donahue2017ddc}; later systems inherit
this template~\cite{takada2023genelive,hanzen2025tcp}. TaikoNation adds
pattern-congruence measures against human charts~\cite{halina2021taikonation}.
None of these metrics has been audited against controlled damage.

\textbf{Generative-music evaluation.} Yang and Lerch compare feature
distributions between generated and reference sets~\cite{yang2020evaluation};
MusPy popularized symbolic statistics~\cite{dong2020muspy}; the Jazz
Transformer introduced self-similarity structureness
indicators~\cite{wu2020jazz}. These are set-level or direction-ambiguous
quantities; we audit their behavior when used as per-chart quality signals.

\textbf{Metric critiques.} Theis et al.\ showed that likelihood and
sample quality can diverge arbitrarily~\cite{theis2016note}; our
blandification result is a sharp corpus-LM instance. NLG research
documented pathologies of distinct-$n$ and
Self-BLEU~\cite{li2016diversity,zhu2018texygen}. Unlike pure critiques we
supply the repair, and unlike human-anchored metric studies our harness
needs no raters. Onset F-measure follows
\texttt{mir\_eval}~\cite{raffel2014mireval}.

\section{Two-Layer Evaluation Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

\textbf{Data.} We use a corpus of 1{,}155 taiko-style chart files
covering 1{,}013 unique songs (grouped by audio identity and normalized
title), split at the song level into 813/80/120
training/validation/test songs so that no song appears in more than one
split. Calibration uses official chart-courses from the training split;
evaluation uses 40 held-out test songs across all difficulty courses.
\PENDING{P9: refresh counts and all downstream numbers from the
clean-split re-run.}

\textbf{Layer 1: raw diagnostics.} Each chart is a sequence of timed,
typed events. We compute quantities with native units and no claimed
direction: note density and local-density percentiles, inter-onset
entropy, color-switch rate, $n$-gram LM negative log-likelihood (trigram,
trained on the calibration corpus), unseen-transition rate,
unique/repeated 4-gram rates, and windowed LM surprisal percentiles.
These deliberately coincide with the borrowed metrics: the audit compares
\emph{normalization strategies}, not measurement choices.

\textbf{Layer 2: calibrated band scores.} For each diagnostic and
difficulty course we estimate a target band $[p_{10},p_{90}]$ over the
calibration corpus. Scores are $1$ inside the band and decay smoothly
outside; error-type quantities use exponential penalties. This resolves
direction ambiguity by construction---``more entropy'' is neither good
nor bad, but ``entropy within the range human charters use at this
difficulty'' is well defined---and it is reference-free at the song
level: scoring a chart for a novel song requires only the fixed corpus
calibration.

\textbf{Role taxonomy.} Scores are assigned roles empirically by the
audit, not a priori: \emph{rankers} respond to damage and separate
systems (pattern-IC, transition validity, repetition and variety
adequacy, rhythm complexity); \emph{constraints} saturate near 1 for all
reasonable charts and flag only gross violations (density, strain,
overload); \emph{diagnostics} carry signal but immature calibration (the
current boredom heuristic; the hypersensitive rare-$n$-gram rate). We
report score profiles, not a single aggregate: fixing trade-off weights
between axes is exactly the judgment we defer to human data.

\caption{Eight corruption probes applied to 167 official charts at three

exactly; C4 and C6 preserve or control density.}
\label{tab:probes}
\end{table}

\tabref{tab:probes} lists the probes. Corruption direction is guaranteed
at the population level: since band scores are two-sided, a corruption
can push an individual off-band chart \emph{toward} its band (measured:
4--14\% of charts per probe at max dose), so all criteria are evaluated
on means over 167 charts. Acceptance requires (a) \emph{sensitivity}:
the designated target score decreases monotonically with dose; (b)
\emph{anti-gaming}: no score family should reward damage; and (c)
\emph{disclosed coupling}: the full probe$\times$metric response matrix
is published rather than summarized by its diagonal.

Fairness rules fixed in advance: identical probes for baselines and
calibrated scores; identity pairs across layers (distinct-4 $\equiv$
unique-4-gram rate; transition validity $\leftrightarrow$
unseen-transition rate) are never counted as independent agreement;
length confounds are checked against note count; and the LM's provenance
is audited (below), with the blandification attack additionally run
\emph{cross-LM} (drive and measure LMs trained on disjoint corpus
halves).

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Sensitivity: targeted damage moves the right scores}
\label{sec:sensitivity}

All six probes with calibrated targets pass: dose--response Spearman
$\rho$ (dose, target score) is negative and significant for C1 rhythm
complexity ($-0.36$), C3 transition validity ($-0.33$), C4 repetition
adequacy ($-0.52$), C6 density/strain adequacy ($-0.65$), C7 density
spike ($-0.78$), and C8 repetition adequacy ($-0.42$), with monotone mean
trajectories (all $p<10^{-15}$). C2's target is the timing family
(\secref{sec:timing}); C5's designated band target holding steady is the
anti-gaming property, reported next.

\subsection{Reductio: borrowed metrics reward damage}
\label{sec:reductio}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figs/fig1_reductio.pdf}
\caption{Borrowed metrics reward damage; calibrated scores respond
correctly. Values are relative to the intact chart (dotted line = no
change); red = borrowed baselines, blue = calibrated scores.
\textbf{Left (C4 loop collapse):} replacing charts with their own most
common 4-gram loop raises structureness by 50\% (0.096$\to$0.144) and
Self-BLEU from 0.860 to 0.892---both read as improvement---while
repetition and variety adequacy correctly fall. \textbf{Right (C5
blandification):} rewriting notes to the LM argmax lowers perplexity by
31\% (7.98$\to$5.48), i.e.\ ``better'' under the standard reading, while
the pattern-IC band score refuses to improve and repetition adequacy
catches the induced looping. The same reversal holds when the attack is
driven by an LM trained on a disjoint corpus half (7.98$\to$5.55).}
\label{fig:reductio}
\end{figure}

\figref{fig:reductio} shows the two reductio exhibits.
\emph{Perplexity rewards blandness}: official charts are more surprising
(perplexity 7.98) than greedy machine output, and flattening human
charts toward the LM's modal patterns drives perplexity \emph{down} by
31\%. Read as ``lower is better''~\cite{donahue2017ddc}, perplexity
prefers the damaged chart---a corpus-LM instance of the
likelihood/quality dissociation~\cite{theis2016note}. The band score
built on the \emph{same} LM statistic is not fooled (0.892$\to$0.900),
because official charts sit near the band's upper edge and flattening
moves NLL toward the band center, not out of it; meanwhile the
repetition-adequacy score catches the damage the attack actually does.
This robustness survives a cross-LM version of the attack (drive on a
held-half LM, measure on the full LM), upgrading it from an in-sample
worst case to evidence that perplexity's failure is not an artifact of
attacking its own model. \emph{Structureness rewards collapse}:
replacing charts with their own dominant 4-gram loop raises
self-similarity structureness~\cite{wu2020jazz} monotonically---copy-paste
is maximally self-similar---and set-level Self-BLEU rises in lockstep.
On real systems the same failures appear in the wild: across eight
generators spanning a known quality range, perplexity separates official
from generated charts with AUC 0.32 (i.e., backwards), and several
borrowed statistics are near-proxies for chart length (Spearman vs.\
note count up to 0.97, vs.\ $\le$0.09 for the calibrated rankers).

\subsection{Closing the anchor blind spot without references}
\label{sec:timing}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figs/fig2_anchor_flip.pdf}
\caption{C2 anchor shift. \textbf{Left:} a constant +15/30/60\,ms shift
of every note against the audio is invisible to all 35 chart-only
metrics (max response 0.0004 SD) \emph{and} to the standard
matching-based offset statistic, which re-matches shifted notes to
neighboring anchors on a dense subdivision lattice ($\approx$14\,ms
spacing at 180\,BPM). The fixed-grid phase offset---support maximization
on an eighth-note lattice anchored to a beat grid \emph{estimated from
audio}---recovers the injected shift to within 0.2\,ms. \textbf{Right:}
the calibrated anchor score collapses from 0.54 to 0.01 across doses.
Results on the 129 charts whose estimated grid passes its own confidence
gate; the estimated grid agrees with metadata grids on period exactly
(31/31 songs) and on phase to $\approx$10\,ms median.}
\label{fig:anchor}
\end{figure}

A chart can be idiomatic on every chart-only axis and still be shifted
off the music entirely---C2 makes this concrete
(\figref{fig:anchor}). Two findings matter beyond the headline. First,
the blindness extends to the obvious fix: a matching-based signed offset
(match each note to its nearest meter anchor, average the residuals) is
itself C2-blind, because a fine subdivision lattice re-matches every
shifted note; detecting global misalignment requires estimating the
phase that maximizes lattice support over a wrap-free window. Second,
the anchors come from a beat grid estimated from audio by a beat-tracking
model, not from any chart metadata, so the timing family is
reference-free like the rest of the protocol; the estimation costs
nothing measurable (period exact on all gated songs, phase agreement
$\approx$10\,ms, matching the estimator's published precision). One
honest caveat: a $\approx$15\,ms grid-phase error on a single song can
saturate that song's unsupported-note rate, so per-song distributions
(not means) should be reported and the phase-offset detector, not the
unsupported rate, is the primary C2 signal.

\subsection{Coupling structure: what specificity we do not claim}
\label{sec:coupling}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figs/fig3_coupling.pdf}
\caption{The full probe$\times$score response matrix (official-SD units
at max dose, clipped at $\pm$4; black boxes = designated targets).
Targeted probes move their targets, but responses are \emph{not}
diagonal: timing jitter (C1) propagates into pattern scores because
pattern tokens are IOI-quantized---in a rhythm game, timing damage
\emph{is} pattern damage---and the rare-$n$-gram diagnostic is
hypersensitive to any token change. We publish the matrix as a coupling
map rather than claiming orthogonality.}
\label{fig:coupling}
\end{figure}

We explicitly do not claim that each probe moves \emph{only} its target.
\figref{fig:coupling} publishes the full response matrix: no probe
passes a strict diagonal-dominance test, for two identifiable reasons.
Shared tokenization couples axes---jittered timings change IOI bins and
therefore every $n$-gram statistic downstream---which is partly domain
truth (mistimed notes damage patterns as played) and partly a suite
limitation. And one diagnostic-tier score (rare-$n$-gram rate) reacts to
almost any token edit, which is why the taxonomy keeps it out of the
ranker tier. Practitioners should read the suite as a coupled profile
with a published response map, not as independent orthogonal axes.

\subsection{Application: scoring real systems}
\label{sec:application}

Applied to eight generators from a controlled ablation family of
conditional chart models (grid-conditioned and gridless decoding modes;
two configurations whose training-budget allocation is known to starve
the gridless task, F-measure 0.58 and 0.36 vs.\ 0.66--0.67 for healthy
configurations), the calibrated profile reproduces the known ordering
with graded response (chart-quality profile 0.83--0.86 healthy, 0.79
semi-collapsed, 0.68 collapsed; official 0.916), while the audio-anchored
family exposes a failure no chart-only score sees: the collapsed system
carries a 38.5\,ms systematic offset against the music (healthy systems
$\approx$10\,ms). Because calibration bands are fixed corpus artifacts,
scores are comparable across studies: re-scoring a two-generations-older
system family under the same bands shows transition validity rising from
0.37 to 0.75--0.82---a cross-generation progress statement that
single-reference F-measure cannot make. Two dissociations illustrate what
F-measure misses: a dedicated gridless model scores higher onset F1 than
a multi-task model (0.673 vs.\ 0.659) yet lower transition validity
(0.753 vs.\ 0.785--0.791), and greedy decoding pushes band-adequacy
scores \emph{above} official values on some axes---band scores reward
in-distribution blandness and must be read jointly with the
variety/repetition axes (which is what the profile is for).

\section{Limitations and Future Work}\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{No human validation.} The probes certify damage detection, not
experience prediction; validating the surviving metrics against player
judgments remains future work.
\textbf{Population-level direction.} 4--14\% of individual charts move
toward their band under corruption; per-chart verdicts need the profile,
not one score.
\textbf{Coupling.} The suite is a coupled profile
(\secref{sec:coupling}); attribution between timing and pattern damage
is smeared by shared tokenization.
\textbf{Scope.} One game domain for the calibration corpus (the
cross-game study covers only the game-agnostic families), greedy
decoding for the real-system study, and course-level (not
level-conditioned) calibration bands.
\textbf{Ceiling.} Among healthy systems the profile differences sit
within evaluation noise; distinguishing good from slightly-better
remains a human question.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Chart generation needs evaluation it can trust more than it needs another
metric. Corruption probes make metric validation systematic and
reproducible: the borrowed metrics did not merely underperform---they
rewarded blandness, rewarded collapse, and missed a fatal global
misalignment entirely.
Corpus-calibrated band scores over the same raw quantities, extended
with an audio-anchored timing family, pass the same probes with
monotone dose response and sub-ms shift recovery, with their coupling
structure published rather than hidden. The harness, calibration
artifacts, per-chart records, and evaluation package are released so
that future chart-quality metrics can be validated under the same
protocol. \PENDING{P8: artifact URL.}

benefits players and small developers; we see no direct misuse vector
beyond automated content farming, which platform moderation already
addresses.
